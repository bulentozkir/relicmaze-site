Milo Move Out: mine-cart puzzles

What’s changed in development build 0.0.17.

Most first-time exits now lead to a 6×6 mine yard. Slide the wagons along their rails to clear a path for Milo’s red cart. Each chamber has its own board; certain routes earn a direct exit instead.

  • Collect each chamber’s treasure as well as its seals before leaving.
  • The Shifting Labyrinth now follows Chamber 90. Chamber 100 still has its Astral Relay, but no Sanctum.

Astral Lantern & Soul Prism

An Astral Lantern phases Milo into the Astral Veil for four seconds so he can walk through sandstone phase walls and cross spirit bridges. Recovering the Soul Prism permanently attunes sight to spectral seams.

5-Slot Tactical Belt, Sun Scepter & Arc Wand

The five-slot belt holds active tools in FIFO order. The Sun Scepter petrifies creatures to harmless stone for six seconds, while the Arc Wand fires a horizontal lightning bolt on Interact to vaporize guardians from afar.

Brazier Torch, Glyph Keys & Zephyr Sandals

A Brazier Torch permanently illuminates pitch-dark lower chambers, color-coded Glyph Keys unlock Cobalt, Jade, Crimson and Solar gates on touch, and Zephyr Sandals boost stride speed and jump reach for eight seconds.

Flooded Basins, Crumbling Slabs & Stone Spheres

Wading in flooded cenotes drains breath until the sluice lever empties the chamber. Crumbling sandstone slabs collapse beneath hurried boots, while giant Bosnian stone spheres thunder down temple chutes.

Hidden Sanctums and crowns

Eleven standalone puzzles sit beyond selected chamber exits. Each chamber offers crowns for clearing it, collecting treasure, and finishing a clean run within par time.

Dying costs nothing

Hit a hazard and you return to the chamber’s entry door. There are no lives to lose, and the seals and one-way levers you’ve already earned stay in place.

The Hill That Looks Back

Some hills look like hills. This one looks back. Visoko is a small town in the green heart of Bosnia, where the rivers Bosna and Fojnica meet. Above its roofs rises a hill called Visočica: 767 metres above the sea and about 213 above the town, with a flank so straight and a crest so sharp that, from the right angle, it looks like a pyramid.

THE LEGEND SAYS

The Pyramid of the Sun is 220 metres tall; later papers say 270, 300 and even 368. The Great Pyramid of Giza is 146. Its four faces slope at about 45 degrees and point to true north, to within an error that one paper puts at twelve seconds of arc.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS

Surveyed, Visočica stands 213 metres above its valley and 767 above the sea. The figures quoted for the base do not agree with one another, and even older texts of the project describe only three well-preserved sides.

ON THE RECORD

For scale: the Great Pyramid of Giza was 146.6 metres tall when it was finished and stands 138.5 today. The hill Visočica, measured from its valley, is taller than that. Whether anything but nature built it is the whole question.

The honest summary: the pyramid is a legend. The valley, its history and the wonder of it are real.
